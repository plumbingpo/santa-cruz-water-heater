Most gas water heaters in Santa Cruz, CA work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve shuts off the gas for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Diagnosing a gas water heater properly means checking combustion, not just parts. Across Santa Cruz, our technicians measure thermocouple output in millivolts, study the pilot and burner flame for color and shape, read gas pressure at the valve, and test draft at the hood so we know exhaust is leaving the house. We check the combustion chamber for soot and scorching, which point to a blocked flue or poor combustion air, and we test for carbon monoxide near the heater. Keep one rule in mind: a gas smell means leaving the house and calling the gas utility from outside before anything else, and Santa Cruz Water Heater can take over from there.

How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver
  • Signs of backdrafting, such as soot, condensation, or an exhaust smell at the vent
  • A faint gas or rotten-egg smell near the heater (leave and call the gas utility)
  • Rust-colored hot water or rust streaks on the tank
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Keep the area around the heater clear, especially of gasoline, paint, and solvents
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Clean dust from around the base so the burner gets plenty of combustion air
