Keeping Your Capitola Water Heater Running Longer

Most early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a surprise cleanup.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Every type of water heater has its own weak points, and we work on all of them in Capitola. Gas tanks tend to have trouble with pilot assemblies and thermocouples, sooty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units burn out heating elements and thermostats, especially when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless units throw error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition faults, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they struggle in cramped closets. We service all four types regularly and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
