Most water heaters do not simply wear out; they rust out or scale up, and both problems can be held off for years. As water heats, dissolved minerals come out of the water and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer sits between the flame and the water, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it builds up around the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, is slowly used up. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Santa Cruz, CA, we see this pattern every week, and most of it was preventable.

Here is what the visit covers. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we verify the expansion tank still holds its air charge, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless water heaters have their own maintenance needs. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers error codes, reduced flow, or temperature swings. We descale the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system
  • Hot water with a sulfur odor
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • No memory of the last time the water heater was serviced
  • Small drips at the drain valve or connections
  • Shorter showers before the water turns lukewarm
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use

Why Professional Water Heater Maintenance Matters

A few maintenance tasks suit homeowners, and many do not. Keeping clutter away from the heater, checking for leaks, and noticing new noises are all useful. Flushing, anode replacement, relief valve testing, and burner or element work are a different matter. Hot water, seized fittings, brittle drain valves, and sediment that clogs the valve midway through can turn a simple job into a flooded floor. Relief valves on older heaters sometimes fail to close after testing. And gas-side checks involve flame quality and venting, which affect your household's air.

We come ready for the common surprises: a drain valve that will not reseal, an anode rod that has turned to wire, a relief valve that drips after testing. We carry replacements, along with meters for element and thermostat testing and a descaling kit for tankless units. More than parts, we bring a trained eye. Small signs, like rust at a connection, a weak flame, or a waterlogged expansion tank, tell us where a heater is headed, and catching them early is the entire point of the visit. Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
  • Consider a water softener if hard water keeps building sediment and scale
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
