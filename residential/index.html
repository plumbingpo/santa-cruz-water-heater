Clear Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Before we pick up a wrench, we figure out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check the age and size of the tank, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any signs of corrosion or seepage. Then you get an honest recommendation and the complete price up front, whether the job is swapping a thermocouple or installing a new heater. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment settles on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod slowly dissolves, and the tank lining starts to rust from the inside with no outward warning. If you notice rusty hot water, rumbling or popping sounds, a rotten-egg smell, shorter hot showers than you're used to, or moisture around the base, call for an inspection sooner rather than later. An early diagnosis usually keeps a fixable issue from becoming a replacement.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most tank water heaters last roughly 8 to 12 years, and a planned water heater replacement is always less expensive and less stressful than an emergency swap with water on the floor. Our technicians know how local water conditions affect tank life, so we can tell you honestly whether your heater has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Each maintenance visit from Santa Cruz Water Heater is more than a quick drain and refill. Our technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, tests the relief valve, checks the burner, pilot, and venting on gas heaters, checks elements and thermostats on electric models, and confirms a safe water temperature. Small problems are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and your water heater stays running clean and efficient.

Most homes do best with a yearly flush, and homes with hard water may need a visit every six months. We adjust the schedule to the age of your equipment, how hard your household runs it, and the mineral content of your water, which controls how fast sediment builds up and how long an anode rod lasts.

Consider maintenance as a safeguard for equipment you've already bought. Each visit helps keep the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod doing its job, and the relief valve ready to open, which lowers monthly energy bills while easing the strain on the burner or elements. For most families, maintenance costs far less than replacing the heater ahead of schedule.

Sized for Your Household

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

An undersized water heater runs out halfway through the second shower, while an oversized one burns fuel keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Santa Cruz Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means looking at the first-hour rating to your morning rush, not just copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the gallons per minute against the fixtures you run at once and how cold your incoming water gets in winter.

Picking a new water heater is about more than tank size. We'll compare tank models with tankless units, and gas with electric or heat pump, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line requirements, electrical capacity, and the installation itself. Tankless works well for many households, though it often requires a bigger gas line and new venting, while a well-sized tank is often the simpler, less expensive choice. The decision is yours, based on your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Santa Cruz

Most repair calls we take come down to a handful of familiar problems. The water never gets hot. The gas burner won't light, or it lights and shuts off. The shower goes cold after a few minutes, the tank rumbles and pops, or the tankless unit locks out with an error code. Add rusty water, sulfur smells, and dripping relief valves, and you have the full list. Our technicians handle all of them, always targeting the component that actually failed.

Some failures can wait a day, and some can't. A bad element only cuts your hot water supply. A relief valve that discharges, on the other hand, is a safety device doing its job and telling you something is wrong, and water pooling under the tank can quietly ruin flooring before anyone notices. If you smell gas, get everyone outside and call the gas utility first. Our technicians carry the parts to swap elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, install expansion tanks where pressure is the problem, and make leak repairs at fittings and drain valves.
