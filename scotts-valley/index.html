Keeping Your Scotts Valley Water Heater Running Longer

Many premature tank failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a flooded utility room.
